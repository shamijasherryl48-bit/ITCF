4.1 Dataset Description 
We obtained the TCGA cohort of renal cell cancer (KIRC, KIRP and KICH) from the Genomic Data Commons 
(GDC) Data Portal [23] that is publicly available. 
Following quality control and multi modal data availability filtering, the final cohort consisted of: 
• 
Clear-cell RCC (KIRC): 511 patients / 512 WSIs 
• 
Papillary RCC (KIRP): 273 patients / 297 WSIs 
• 
Chromophobe RCC (KICH): 109 patients / 121 WSIs 
• 
Total No. of patients: 893 , (930 whole-slide images). 
Inclusion criteria: 
Diagnostic FFPE H&E whole-slide image, somatic mutation data for reliable TMB calculation, and basic clinical 
variables (age, sex and tumour stage). 
Exclusion criteria: 
Failure to obtain the diagnostic slide, artefacts on the slide, quality of the slides (insufficient tumour material), 
samples without mutation calling data or lack of survival data (when required for Cox regression analysis). 
Immunotherapy-response labels: 
TCGA-RCC does not systematically collect patient-level data on immune-checkpoint inhibitor exposure, 
treatment regimen, RECIST response or duration of response. Hence, the clinical immunotherapy response is not 
directly used as a supervised learning target in the present work. TMB is rather considered as a continuous 
molecular feature and the resulting score is merely provided as a research informative feature based on TMB and 
not as a validated clinical prediction of response. Table 1 shows the distribution of TMB in TCGA-RCC subtypes.

Tumour Mutational Burden (TMB) was measured as the number of non-synonymous somatic mutations per 
megabase of the coding region, using the TCGA Multi-Center Mutation Calling in Multiple Cancers (MC3) public 
MAF files (whole-exome sequencing). The approximation of the denominator of the capture size was around 38 
Mb. However, the unit is mutations per megabase (mut/Mb). A threshold of TMB ≥ 10 mut/Mb was only used in 
binary visualisation (Figure 4) and was not used to define a clinical response class for immunotherapy. 
Additionally, mutations that were commonly found in RCC were included as key driver mutations. VHL is a 
tumour-suppressor gene that is mutated in most clear-cell RCCs, and it plays a role in the hypoxia pathway. 
PBRM1 codes for a subunit of the SWI/SNF chromatin-remodelling complex and is the second gene most 
frequently mutated in clear-cell RCC. MET is a proto-oncogene that is often amplified and mutated in papillary 
RCC. 
The following was used as a continuous genomic feature for TMB: 
(1) RCC subtype classification, and 
(2) development of a continuous research surrogate score based on TMB. 
Missing genomic values were handled using k-nearest neighbour imputation (kNNI). Clinical variables with 
missing values were handled by complete-case analysis. 
4.1.1 Experimental Setup and Data Partitioning 
All data partitioning was done at the patient level only to avoid data leakage. All whole slide images or patches 
from the same patient were always mapped to the same partition. The whole TCGA-RCC cohort (893 patients) 
was split randomly into a training group of 714 patients (80%) and an independent held-out test group of 179 
patients (20%) at the patient level. Hyperparameter tuning and model selection were conducted using only the 
714-patient training set via 5-fold cross validation. The held-out test set was not used in training, hyperparameter 
optimization or model selection and was only evaluated once at the end after the final model was chosen. The 
distribution of the Patient-level subtypes is illustrated in Table 2 for the training and held-out test sets.

4.4 Quantitative analysis 
Table 3 shows the univariate and multivariable Cox proportional hazard analyses for overall survival (OS). In the 
univariate analysis, age (HR = 1.019, 95% CI: 1.012–1.026; P < 0.001), advanced tumour stage (HR = 1.878, 95% 
CI: 1.619–2.180; P < 0.001), tumour mutational burden (TMB; HR = 1.766, 95% CI: 1.662–1.878; P < 0.001), 
SETD2 mutation (HR = 1.243, 95% CI: 1.014–1.524; P = 0.036), MET mutation (HR = 1.457, 95% CI: 1.084–
1.959; P = 0.013), and the ITCF Surrogate Score (HR = 4.361, 95% CI: 3.696–5.144; P < 0.001) were significantly 
associated with OS. Univariate analysis showed that sex, VHL mutation, PBRM1 mutation or BAP1 mutation 
were not statistically significant. In the multivariable analysis, age (HR = 1.026, 95% CI: 1.019–1.033; P < 0.001), 
advanced tumour stage (HR = 1.504, 95% CI: 1.289–1.754; P < 0.001), SETD2 mutation (HR = 1.328, 95% CI: 
1.080–1.634; P = 0.007), and the ITCF Surrogate Score (HR = 4.383, 95% CI: 3.679–5.222; P < 0.001) remained 
statistically significant. After adjustment for the other covariates in the model, sex, VHL mutation, PBRM1 
mutation, BAP1 mutation and MET mutation were not statistically significant. As the ITCF Surrogate Score 
already includes information related to TMB, the multivariable model did not include information on TMB as an 
additional covariate to avoid potential multicollinearity between the variables. To assess the individual association 
with OS, TMB was kept in the univariate analysis. The results showed that the ITCF Surrogate Score was related 
to OS, after controlling for the covariates included. It is clarifying that this association is a survival association 
and cannot be considered as a clinical utility or predict the treatment response.

4.6 External Validation 
The CPTAC-CCRCC cohort was then used as an external cohort to assess the generalizability of the proposed 
Integrated Transformer–Contrastive Framework (ITCF) for survival assessment [24]. No retraining or fine-tuning 
was done on the external data and external evaluation was done. To evaluate the generalizability of the survival-
prediction part of ITCF to the TCGA-RCC development cohort, external prognostic evaluation was carried out 
on the CPTAC-CCRCC cohort. The patients who were eligible were those for whom input models were available 
and where annotations of overall survival (survival time, and vital status) were available. Trained model was not 
retrained or finetuned. The Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, log rank test, and Cox proportional-hazards regression 
were used to evaluate the prognostic performance. Patients were divided into high-risk and low-risk groups based 
on their risk prediction scores. Time-dependent AUCs and Harrell's concordance index were also performed.

4.7 Survival Analysis 
Survival was analyzed using a standard Cox model and a Kaplan–Meier analysis. There was no covariate that did 
not meet the proportional hazards assumption, so the model was not time-dependent. Patients were divided into 
two groups: high-risk and low-risk groups based on the median of the surrogate score. 
When examining the entire TCGA-RCC cohort, the high-risk group had significantly poorer overall survival than 
the low-risk group (log-rank P < 0.001). This unadjusted comparison is a hazard ratio of 2.47 (1.89 - 3.23). It is 
NOT the multivariate answer. The median follow-up period was 48.6 months. The risk table is based on 450 and 
443 patients, adding up to the total population of 893.
